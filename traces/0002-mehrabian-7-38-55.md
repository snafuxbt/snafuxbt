# 0002: The 7-38-55 "rule"

**Verdict:** distorted. The numbers come from real 1967 experiments by Albert Mehrabian, but they describe how people judge *liking vs disliking* from single words, not communication in general.

## Chain
1. **Mehrabian & Ferris, "Inference of attitudes from nonverbal communication in two channels", Journal of Consulting Psychology 31(3), 1967.** 62 female undergraduates took part across three groups; 20 rated the combined face + voice stimuli (36 conditions). The only spoken word was "maybe". The fitted equation covers face and voice only: AT = 1.50 AF + 1.03 AV. The last page says: "It is suggested that the combined effect of simultaneous verbal, vocal, and facial attitude communications is a weighted sum of their independent effects ... with the coefficients of .07, .38, and .55, respectively." That is a proposal combining this study with Mehrabian & Wiener (1967), not a measurement in this paper. [PDF](https://ibralc.com.br/wp-content/uploads/sites/5/2026/01/mehrabian-1967-inference-of-attitudes-from-nonverbal-communication.pdf)
2. **Mehrabian's own website.** "Total Liking = 7% Verbal Liking + 38% Vocal Liking + 55% Facial Liking" and "Unless a communicator is talking about their feelings or attitudes, these equations are not applicable." [Page](http://www.kaaj.com/psych/smorder.html)
3. **Lapakko, "Communication is 93% Nonverbal: An Urban Legend Proliferates", 2015.** Says the figures derive "from two 1967 studies with serious methodological limitations" and are spread online with "little or no understanding of the research that generated these numbers". [DOI](https://doi.org/10.56816/2471-0032.1000)
